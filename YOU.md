# You

The rest of this corpus is advice on how to do software engineering. I am qualified to give that advice, and the documents defend it. This one is different, and it opens with a different disclaimer: **I am not qualified to tell you who to be.** No one is. What I can do is show you who I strive to be: my values, my Whys, and the high-priority Whats and Hows I derive from them ([Why, What, How](WHY-WHAT-HOW.md), applied to a person). Beyond the README, it is the only document here written in the first person, because it could not honestly be written any other way.

You will recognize the four principles in what follows: [What Matters Most](WHAT-MATTERS-MOST.md) is these values, turned to face the work.

## My Whys

Who I strive to be:

* **Kindness.** Kindness isn't just avoiding harm to others; it is based in recognition and respect. Kindness means you treat others as you yourself wish to be treated, not because that is the golden rule, but because they are full and complete beings who have lived their own journeys, faced their own hardships, learned their own truths. Kindness means recognizing and respecting their inherent worth and wisdom. Kindness includes crediting someone for their successes.

* **Integrity** (which requires courage) is telling the truth (of course) and without omission, but never with cruelty. It is making only promises you can keep, and keeping the promises you make. It is playing fair, even when no one would see you cheat, even when others are cheating. It is doing the whole job and doing it right, even if cutting corners would make it faster or easier or cheaper. It is telling the rest of the world when some accomplishment is the work of another person (and see Kindness, above). It is living the life that makes you someone else's hero. It is being the person your dog thinks you are.

* **Courage** is doing right, even when right is the hard path. Even if you have to stand up to everyone else who wants to do the wrong thing. It takes courage to change, to admit mistakes, to try something completely new. Courage enables integrity and growth and even kindness.

* **Growth, Learning, and Curiosity.** The more you know about a topic, the more you realize how much you **don't** know. The ability to add new truths to your understanding, and, when those new truths disagree with your current opinions, the strength to change your opinion, admit you were wrong, and then act on your new knowledge ... that is how we grow. Not only is there much we don't yet know; the world is changing fast, constantly adding more **to** know. Stay curious. Always a student, never a master.

* **Competence.** I strive to excel at what I do. I want to be useful. I want to create work of which I can be proud. I want my work to do the intended job, at a cost that satisfies the problem, and yet be robust enough to continue performing well beyond expectations. I want to be good enough that I can be a model to others.

## My Whats and Hows

If those are my Whys, then these are some high-priority Whats and Hows, each traced to the Whys it serves:

* I have and know my personal goals. I look at them every day. I take steps toward them every day. As I plan, a constant question is: how does this serve my goals? (Growth.)

* Start! Even if I can only bite off a small piece and start that. (Courage? Competence?)

* Ask the right questions. Keep asking them. (Courage. Competence. Integrity? Curiosity?)

* Always know "the definition of done." Spending too much effort on one thing is just as bad as (and usually includes) spending too little effort on some other important thing. (Competence.)

* Simplify: simplify your design, simplify your workflow, simplify your plans, simplify your prose. (Competence.)

* Don't rush. Plan better instead. Don't push through and work tired. Sleep, and start again in the morning. In both cases, the results are vastly better, both in the work you produce and in your own health. (Competence. Integrity?)

* Get a little bit better every day. Growth is constant and slow. Mistakes mustn't stop you; they are a learning opportunity. A piece of chocolate cake doesn't end your diet. It's a mistake to learn and recover from. (Growth. Learning.)

* Push hard, practice hard: hard enough to make you grow. (Competence. Growth. Learning.)

* Set expectations. (Integrity. Competence?)

* Plan realistically: it's harder than you think, unforeseen obstacles crop up, and you will be working on many tasks at once; this is just one. Some things you will have to do are going to be awful, but you must do them just the same. Prioritize smartly. Know when to say "No." Don't plan to 100%; plan to 80% and follow your priorities (including the things you wish you could avoid). (Competence. Integrity. Courage?)

* Take notes. Remember what you decided **against**, and why. Remember what you accomplished. Remember how long it actually took vs. how long you planned. Remember what things you want to get done in the future. (Competence.)

These are mine. Yours will be different, but it is worth knowing who you strive to be.
